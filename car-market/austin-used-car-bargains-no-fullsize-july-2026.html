Methodology & Truck Exclusion

Produced by the same multi-agent harness as the first report: parallel collection agents fetched live listings from the accessible Austin-metro sources across body styles, followed by a high-reasoning screen-and-rank pass. The ranking screen rejected any full-size, heavy-duty, or mid-size pickup (only compact unibody trucks kept) and any van.

  • Excluded — full-size / heavy-duty: Ford F-150 and F-250/F-350+, Chevrolet Silverado 1500 and 2500/3500+, GMC Sierra 1500+, RAM 1500 and 2500/3500+, Toyota Tundra, Nissan Titan, and the body-on-frame Chevrolet Suburban.
  • Excluded — mid-size: Chevrolet Colorado, GMC Canyon, Ford Ranger, Toyota Tacoma, Nissan Frontier, Honda Ridgeline.
  • Excluded — vans: all cargo/passenger vans (e.g. Ford Transit, Chevrolet Express, RAM ProMaster).
  • Kept — compact unibody trucks only: Ford Maverick, Hyundai Santa Cruz.
  • Private listings require a VIN. For-sale-by-owner listings that don't publish a VIN were excluded (dealer listings expose a VIN on the detail page); four no-VIN Craigslist listings were dropped on this basis.
  • Discount: fair local market value estimated at the model/trim/mileage level for Austin, then discount % = (est. market value − price) ÷ est. market value. Platform deal badges corroborate only.
  • Demotion: discounts near or above ~20% are demoted as more likely to signal a problem than a deal.

Pre-Purchase Screening Checklist

Every pick above is a candidate, not a verified clean car. None of these platforms expose title status at the search level, and a clean-looking title can itself be washed. Before buying — especially the higher-mileage trucks, the fleet-mileage van, and any luxury vehicle — run this checklist:

  • Pull a full VIN history report (Carfax or AutoCheck) to rule out salvage, rebuilt, flood, and branded titles — title washing can erase a salvage brand by re-registering out of state.
  • Run the free NICB VINCheck and an NMVTIS-approved provider for theft and total-loss records.
  • Verify the VIN matches across the dashboard plate, driver's-door jamb, title, and history report — mismatches signal VIN cloning.
  • Get an independent pre-purchase mechanical inspection before any money changes hands.
  • Watch for curbstoning on private sales: the same phone on multiple listings, meeting in a parking lot rather than a home, or a title not in the seller's name.
  • Treat an unusually large discount as a warning: if the price is far below every comparable local listing, assume there's a reason until the VIN report and inspection prove otherwise.

Caveats & Limitations

  • Market values are model/trim/mileage-level estimates for the Austin metro, not VIN-specific appraisals. Condition, options, accident history, and title status can move true value materially.
  • Discounts near or above ~20% deserve suspicion — they more often signal deferred maintenance, undisclosed damage, or a branded title than a true bargain.
  • Craigslist private listings are the thinnest source — most show no VIN and several show no mileage, so those discounts are provisional.
  • Deal badges corroborate only — they were not treated as independent valuations.
  • Point-in-time snapshot. Prices/availability change quickly; links were verified July 17, 2026 but may expire as inventory turns over.
  • No purchase should proceed without a VIN history report and an independent inspection — see the screening checklist.
